# Connected Case Organisation

**Name:** Meridian EcoPack Pte Ltd (MEP)

**Profile:** a fictional Singapore SME with 120 employees producing printed corrugated and fibre-based packaging for regional food, electronics and healthcare customers.

**Customers:** Two key customers require evidence of an ISO 14001:2026-aligned EMS and stronger value-chain environmental controls before contract renewal.

**Symptoms:** Electricity intensity rose 11%; mixed production waste increased; an ink tote spill nearly reached a drain; wastewater and contractor records are incomplete; nearby tenants reported intermittent odour; supplier environmental evidence varies.

**Goal:** Build an evidence-led EMS that protects the environment, meets applicable obligations, improves performance and supports certification readiness.

# 1. EMS Foundations and ISO 14001

An environmental management system (EMS) is the connected set of policies, responsibilities, processes, resources, controls and evidence used to manage environmental aspects, meet compliance obligations and improve environmental performance. It should be integrated with normal management rather than operated as a separate certificate project.

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Family document** | **Purpose in an EMS** |
| ISO 14001 | Auditable EMS requirements with guidance for use. |
| ISO 14004 | General guidance for phased EMS establishment, implementation, maintenance and improvement. |
| ISO 14031 | Guidance on environmental performance evaluation. |
| ISO 14040/14044 | Principles, framework and requirements for life-cycle assessment. |
| ISO 19011 | Guidance for management-system audit programmes and audits. |

## Core working terms

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Term** | **Working distinction** | **MEP example** |
| Environmental aspect | Element of an activity, product or service that interacts or can interact with the environment. | Ink storage, electricity use or paper offcuts. |
| Environmental impact | Change to the environment resulting wholly or partly from an aspect. | Water contamination, emissions or resource depletion. |
| Environmental condition | State or characteristic of the environment at a point in time. | Flood exposure, air quality, water stress or sensitive receptors. |
| Hazard and risk | A hazard is a potential source of harm; risk considers uncertainty and consequence. EMS planning also considers opportunities. | A spill pathway to a drain and the likelihood/consequence of pollution. |
| Compliance obligation | Legal requirement or other requirement an organisation has to or chooses to comply with. | Permit condition, applicable regulation or customer commitment. |
| Environmental performance | Measurable results related to management of environmental aspects. | kWh per tonne, wastewater result or waste-diversion rate. |

## PDCA and intended outcomes

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PDCA** | **Management question** | **Typical EMS evidence** |
| Plan | What context, aspects, obligations, risks, opportunities and objectives matter? | Context review, registers and plans. |
| Do | Are competent people applying life-cycle and operational controls? | Operating criteria, supplier controls, competence and emergency arrangements. |
| Check | What happened, are obligations fulfilled and is the EMS effective? | Monitoring, compliance evaluation, audit and management review. |
| Act | What must be corrected, improved or changed? | Corrective action, effectiveness review and improvement decisions. |

Certification is third-party assurance of an organisation's defined EMS scope; ISO does not certify organisations. A typical journey includes leadership commitment, gap analysis, implementation and evidence accumulation, internal audit and management review, certification audit stages, corrective action and surveillance.

## A defensible aspect-significance method

1. Define the process, activity, product/service, boundary and operating condition.
2. Identify inputs, outputs, releases, resource use, waste and credible emergencies.
3. Write the aspect as the interaction/cause and the impact as the environmental change.
4. Apply documented criteria such as severity, likelihood, compliance exposure, stakeholder concern and control effectiveness.
5. Apply explicit override rules for legal, emergency or stakeholder triggers.
6. Separate inherent significance from residual risk after controls; record uncertainty and evidence.
7. Link priorities to controls, objectives, monitoring and improvement.
8. Review after incidents, change, new evidence, changing obligations or planned intervals.

|  |
| --- |
| **Inside-out and outside-in** Inside-out analysis asks how MEP affects the environment. Outside-in analysis asks how environmental conditions such as climate, biodiversity, resource availability or flooding affect MEP's ability to achieve EMS outcomes. Both inform context and planning. |

## Compliance obligations workflow

1. Identify statutory, regulatory, permit, contract, customer and voluntary sources.
2. Verify currency and applicability with authoritative sources and competent advice where needed.
3. Link a plain-language applicability statement to a process and aspect.
4. Define control, evidence, owner, frequency and escalation.
5. Periodically evaluate compliance and record evidence, status and evaluator.
6. Monitor changes and update controls, competence and information.

| Emergency readiness | All potential emergencies are identified, prevented where possible, rehearsed and improved. | Are roles, equipment, receptors, recovery and learning covered? |

## Life-cycle perspective without requiring a full LCA

* Consider design/material selection, purchasing, operations, delivery/use where influenced and end-of-life.
* Distinguish direct control from influence through specifications, purchasing, contracts or guidance.
* Use proportionate environmental criteria and evidence for external providers.
* Avoid shifting impacts between stages—for example, lighter packaging that increases damage.

## Emergency preparedness and response

1. Identify credible emergency situations and receptors/pathways.
2. Prevent or mitigate adverse impacts where practicable.
3. Define authority, roles, alarms, containment, communications, notification assessment, recovery and evidence protection.
4. Provide equipment and competent people; coordinate contractors/responders.
5. Test arrangements periodically where practicable and after change.
6. Evaluate tests or events; correct gaps and retain learning.

# 4. Performance Evaluation — Clause 9

Monitoring generates observations. Measurement assigns values. Analysis identifies patterns. Evaluation compares evidence with criteria to support a decision. Keeping these distinct prevents a dashboard from being mistaken for a compliance or effectiveness conclusion.

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Element** | **Design questions** | **Example evidence** |
| Indicators | What, method, unit/boundary, frequency, owner, threshold and data-quality control? | kWh per tonne, wastewater result, diversion and control completion. |
| Compliance evaluation | Which obligation/evidence, by whom, when and with what status/action? | Evaluation record, source/version, sample and escalation. |
| Internal audit | What objectives, scope, criteria, methods, independence and risk-informed sampling? | Programme, plan, evidence log, findings and follow-up. |
| Management review | What changes, trends, objectives, compliance, audits, resources and opportunities require decisions? | Minutes with changes, resources, owners, dates and follow-up. |

## Writing a defensible audit finding

* Criterion: identify the applicable requirement or organisational control.
* Objective evidence: state verifiable facts, sample and references.
* Gap: explain the difference without prescribing a solution.
* Risk/context: describe credible consequence and scope.
* Classification: apply defined programme rules consistently.

# 5. Improvement and ISO 14001:2026 Transition — Clause 10

|  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Response** | **Purpose** | **MEP example** |
| Correction | Control or correct the detected issue. | Replace obsolete contractor contacts. |
| Containment | Protect receptors, processes and evidence. | Isolate the drain, stop release and secure affected material. |
| Corrective action | Eliminate verified cause and reduce recurrence. | Redesign ownership/change controls for emergency readiness. |
| Effectiveness review | Test sustained intended results. | Sample shifts and repeat a timed drill against criteria. |
| Continual improvement | Improve EMS and environmental performance. | Use trend, audit, incident and review evidence. |

## A controlled 2026 transition

1. Confirm transition arrangements with the accredited certification body.
2. Perform a clause/process gap review using a licensed ISO 14001:2026 copy.
3. Prioritise environmental/compliance risk, dependencies and evidence maturity.
4. Address environmental conditions, outcomes, leadership support, planned change, value-chain oversight, all potential emergencies, audit objectives and management-review results.
5. Assign owners, resources, milestones and competence/document changes.
6. Implement without weakening existing controls; verify effectiveness.
7. Complete internal audit and management review before the transition audit.

## Activity A1 — Build the Environmental Baseline and Aspect Inventory (A1)

Identify workplace environmental hazards and aspects from process and site evidence.

|  |
| --- |
| **Scenario** MEP is preparing its first ISO 14001:2026-aligned EMS. Managers have energy bills and waste invoices, but no shared view of where activities interact with air, water, land, resources or neighbours. |

**Expected output:** A process-boundary map, environmental baseline and aspect-impact inventory with evidence references.

**Files:** Scenario guide PDF, EMS Evidence Workbook (Excel), site-plan evidence cards

### Decision questions

1. Which activities, products and services create direct or influenced environmental interactions?
2. For each interaction, what is the aspect and what is the resulting environmental impact?
3. What normal, abnormal and emergency conditions must be included, and what evidence is still missing?

### Detailed step-by-step procedure

**Step 1:** Read the company profile, process flow, site plan and evidence cards before scoring anything.

**How to complete and evidence:** Read all evidence and record source, date and reviewer before deciding.

**Step 2:** Define the review boundary from receipt of paper and inks through printing, conversion, storage, dispatch and end-of-life influence.

**How to complete and evidence:** Complete the intended workbook fields in plain language; label assumptions and missing data.

**Step 3:** Walk each process stage and record inputs, outputs, releases, resource use, waste streams and potential emergencies.

**How to complete and evidence:** Apply the supplied criteria or decision rule consistently and retain a repeatable rationale.

**Step 4:** Write each aspect as a cause or interaction and each impact as a change to the environment.

**How to complete and evidence:** Make the decision explicit: control, priority, owner, date, threshold, escalation or follow-up.

**Step 5:** Tag the operating condition, control/influence level, evidence source, owner and missing data for every entry.

**How to complete and evidence:** Ask a peer to challenge one material assumption or unintended consequence and record the response.

**Step 6:** Peer-review for omissions at maintenance, contractors, utilities, transport and product end-of-life stages.

**How to complete and evidence:** Reconcile the output with earlier activities and update linked evidence to prevent conflicting versions.

### Acceptance test

The inventory covers normal, abnormal and potential emergency conditions; every aspect-impact pair is logically linked and traceable to supplied evidence or an explicit evidence gap.

* The workbook is complete, legible and uses the intended tabs.
* Every conclusion is traceable to evidence or labelled as an assumption/evidence gap.
* Owners, dates, measures, controls and next decisions are stated where applicable.
* The team can explain the PDCA link, ability code and environmental consequence.

### Debrief and submission

* Which activities, products and services create direct or influenced environmental interactions?
* For each interaction, what is the aspect and what is the resulting environmental impact?
* What normal, abnormal and emergency conditions must be included, and what evidence is still missing?
* What evidence would most improve confidence?
* What will trigger review or escalation?

## Activity A7 — Evaluate Performance and Conduct an Internal Audit (A5, A6)

Evaluate environmental performance, compliance evidence and EMS effectiveness through risk-based audit sampling.

|  |
| --- |

**Expected output:** A KPI interpretation, compliance-evaluation record, audit plan, evidence log and defensible findings.

**Files:** Performance and Audit Workbook (Excel), audit role-play evidence pack and checklist PDF

### Decision questions

1. What can the data support, what is uncertain and what requires normalisation or validation?
2. How is evaluation of compliance different from routine monitoring and from internal audit?
3. Does each finding state the criterion, objective evidence and gap without prescribing a solution?

### Detailed step-by-step procedure

**Step 1:** Verify KPI definitions, boundaries, units, calibration/data-quality controls and missing-data treatment.

**How to complete and evidence:** Read all evidence and record source, date and reviewer before deciding.

**Step 2:** Normalise energy, water and waste measures to production where appropriate and interpret trends against thresholds.

**How to complete and evidence:** Complete the intended workbook fields in plain language; label assumptions and missing data.

**Step 3:** Evaluate a sample of obligations using current evidence and record status, basis, evaluator and action.

**How to complete and evidence:** Apply the supplied criteria or decision rule consistently and retain a repeatable rationale.

**Step 4:** Set audit objectives, scope, criteria, methods, timing and independence using risk and prior performance.

**How to complete and evidence:** Make the decision explicit: control, priority, owner, date, threshold, escalation or follow-up.

**Step 5:** Follow one aspect from policy and planning through operation, monitoring, competence and improvement evidence.

### Acceptance test

Conclusions respect data limitations; compliance status is evidence-based; the audit trail is reproducible; every finding contains criterion, evidence and gap; training follow-up is tied to observed competence needs.

# Glossary

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Term** | **Working definition** |
