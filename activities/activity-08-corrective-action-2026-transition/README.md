# Activity A8 - Close Corrective Action and Plan the 2026 Transition

**Course:** Fundamentals of ISO 14001 Environmental Management System (EMS) (TGS-2023037592)  
**Ability:** A7, A8  
**Objective:** Document nonconformity, root cause, corrective action, effectiveness and controlled improvement.  
**Mode:** Team analysis with individual evidence submission  
**Suggested time:** 60 minutes

## Scenario

The tabletop showed that drain isolation ownership was unclear and contractor contact data was obsolete. Leadership also needs a structured gap review for the 2026 edition, including value-chain oversight and planned change.

Meridian EcoPack Pte Ltd (MEP) is a fictional Singapore SME with 120 employees producing printed corrugated and fibre-based packaging for regional food, electronics and healthcare customers. Two key customers require evidence of an ISO 14001:2026-aligned EMS and stronger value-chain environmental controls before contract renewal.

## Task and expected output

Close the spill-response weakness and convert ISO 14001:2026 gaps into a prioritised transition roadmap.

Produce: **An NCR/CAPA record, effectiveness review, continual-improvement report and controlled transition plan.**

## Before you begin

- Open a working copy of the Excel workbook; do not overwrite the supplied evidence.
- Allocate a facilitator, evidence recorder, checker and presenter.
- Read every evidence card before making a compliance or significance conclusion.
- If the activity concerns an incident, this is a tabletop exercise: do not handle chemicals or simulate unsafe physical actions.

## Decision questions

1. What is the immediate correction, what is the verified systemic cause and what similar risks exist elsewhere?
2. Which measures will demonstrate that the action remains effective after enough evidence has accumulated?
3. Which 2026 changes affect MEP's EMS, and how will the transition avoid disrupting current controls?

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Actions address verified causes, transition priorities are traceable to current requirements, effectiveness is measured after an appropriate interval, and a reviewer can reconstruct decisions, ownership, evidence and residual risk.
- [ ] Claims are traceable to supplied or cited evidence.
- [ ] Assumptions, uncertainty and missing evidence are labelled.
- [ ] Owners, due dates and next decisions are included where applicable.
- [ ] Legal/compliance interpretations needing competent advice are escalated rather than guessed.
- [ ] A peer challenge and response are recorded.

## Troubleshooting

- **The team cannot agree on a score:** return to the criterion anchors and record each evidence-based assumption before averaging or escalating.
- **Evidence is missing:** do not invent it; record an evidence gap, risk implication, owner and retrieval action.
- **A legal requirement is unclear:** record the current authoritative source and escalate to a competent adviser or authority channel.
- **A conclusion feels obvious:** test an alternative explanation and state what evidence would falsify the current view.

## Facilitator debrief

Ask each team to defend one priority, control or conclusion using objective evidence. Probe what would change the decision, then connect the work to PDCA and the relevant EMS process rather than clause memorisation.
